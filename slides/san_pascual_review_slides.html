San Pascual Cell-Site Review

The proposed tower in deeper Barangay Bolod near the Pinamasingan Road, tested against the existing Bolod hill towers and against road-accessible ridge sites, using a coverage model calibrated to what residents actually receive.

Revision 2 · October 2026 · Field-calibrated coverage model (usable range 3–4 km)
Summary

The proposed tower copies Bolod. Ridge sites reach new people.

36,021residents in 18 barangays with no usable signal (81% of 44,449)
87%of the contractor site's coverage is already covered by Bolod
0unserved residents it adds in all three location scenarios with a 40 m mast (at most 15 even at 60 m)
4,314added by the best single ridge site (Malaking Ilog–Mapanique)

What we found

  • The existing Bolod towers reach about 3–4 km. They serve Bolod, Terraplin, Nazareno and Busing, and do not reach Laurente. Our model now reproduces this.
  • The contractor's antennas would sit 46 m lower than Bolod's, 1–2 km away. Everything they can reach, Bolod already reaches.
  • Because signal only travels 3–4 km here, the unserved barangays need towers near them, on high ground. Every ridge site we propose is within 1 km of a road and has a clear microwave link back.

What we recommend

  • Do not endorse the deeper-Bolod site as a coverage project.
  • Ask for the Malaking Ilog–Mapanique and San Rafael–Santa Cruz ridges first: +8,403 residents, taking service from 20% to 39%.
  • If the contractor will only build on its own road: the Pinamasingan ridge (+2,615) or the Cueva ridge (+2,813).
  • Ask for an eight-site roadmap: 71% of residents within service.

Model review

What changed in this revision

1. Laurente

The first map showed Bolod reaching parts of Laurente. Field reports say it does not. The terrain confirms it: a spur rises about 41 m above the radio path, 3.7 km out.

Now: Laurente 1% covered.

2. Range

The first model used a low-band, open-terrain budget and let signal run 10 km or more. Residents report usable signal ends at about 3–4 km.

Now: median land range 3.2 km (most directions 2.8–3.45 km).

3. The map itself

The first maps used one flat colour over a grey relief, which read like a flood-depth map and hid how strong the signal is.

Now: graded RSRP classes from excellent to edge, on a contour basemap with 1–4 km range rings, land only.

All conclusions were recomputed. The direction of the finding is unchanged and stronger: the contractor's site adds zero unserved residents. Because each tower now reaches less, more towers are needed, and each must sit close to the people it serves.

The model

  • Terrain: SRTM 30 m elevation, radar spikes removed.
  • Path loss: COST-231 Hata (suburban) at 1800 MHz, plus terrain diffraction over the actual ridges (Deygout, ITU-R P.526).
  • Effective antenna height above each receiver, so hilltop sites get their real advantage.
  • Handset uplink fade beyond 3.5 km, matching the observed 3–4 km reach.
  • Thresholds: ≥ −110 dBm usable outdoors, ≥ −100 dBm indoors.

The free parameters were tuned so Bolod hill covers its four barangays and nothing else, ending at 3–4 km. A drive test should replace this tuning before final siting.

Decision matrix

The commercial picture, read honestly

Contractor's site

No new subscribers. All revenue is traffic moved off Bolod. That justifies a capacity upgrade, which co-location on the existing Bolod towers delivers without a new structure.

Single ridge site

About 4,314 new residents at best: roughly ₱2.6–3.9 M a year against ₱13–21 M capex. Payback is about 4–7 years, slower than an urban site but positive, and it opens a captive market.

Shared network

With a 3–4 km reach, full coverage needs several sites. Multi-operator sharing, DICT common-tower and connectivity programmes, and LGU help with land and access roads are what make sites N3–N8 viable.

The council's leverage is simple: offer fast permitting, land and road help for ridge sites, and ask for nothing new for a site that adds no one.

Hearing strategy

Check the council's legal footing first

Executive Order No. 32 (s. 2023) lists Sanggunian resolutions among the requirements that are no longer prerequisites for shared telecom tower permits. Revised JMC No. 01 s. 2021 bars LGUs from requiring documents beyond its list.

Before the hearing

  • Ask the Municipal Legal Officer and DILG provincial office whether this RONO is legally required, or a courtesy endorsement the contractor is seeking.
  • If a courtesy: write the conditions into a Memorandum of Agreement the contractor signs.

Levers the LGU still holds

  • Municipal and barangay land for ridge sites, and right-of-way for access spurs.
  • Building-permit technical review, zoning and locational clearance.
  • Fast one-stop-shop processing under EO 32.
  • A council resolution of findings to DICT and NTC.

Hearing strategy

Five questions for the councilors to ask

  1. Q1

    "Show us your RSRP propagation map, for your site and for the existing Bolod towers, and the difference."

    Ask for −100 and −110 dBm contours, the model and terrain data, antenna height, azimuth, tilt and power. Then: which barangays, and how many residents, gain service they do not have today?

  2. Q2

    "Your antennas will be about 46 m lower than the ones already on Bolod hill. Name one unserved barangay you will reach that Bolod cannot."

    Ask for the terrain profile to Pinamasingan, Laurente or San Rafael. Ask whether they evaluated the Pinamasingan ridge on the same road, and for their site-search ring.

  3. Q3

    "Which operator will be on this tower, and why not co-locate on the existing Bolod towers?"

    If the purpose is a new operator or more capacity, DICT's co-location policy points to the existing structures.

  4. Q4

    "What is your written commitment to the 18 unserved barangays, with sites and dates?"

    Ask for a rollout schedule with coordinates and on-air dates, and a drive test shared with the council within 60 days of switching on.

  5. Q5

    "Which frequency band will the new tower use, and will it stay up when the grid is down or a typhoon hits?"

    A sub-GHz layer (700/850 MHz) reaches further than the 3–4 km seen today. Ask for battery hours, generator or solar backup, the NSCP wind rating and typhoon restoration time.

Hearing strategy

Conditions before granting no objection

Coverage extension

  • The site must give RSRP ≥ −100 dBm to at least [2,000] residents of unserved barangays not reached from Bolod. ALT-A to ALT-E pre-qualify, subject to survey.
  • If they insist on deeper Bolod: capacity only, tied to a ridge site on air within [12–18] months, or the endorsement lapses.
  • For a new tenant or capacity: show first why co-location on Bolod is impossible.

Evidence first

  • Propagation study (Longley-Rice or ITU-R P.1812, terrain ≤ 30 m) for the site, Bolod and the difference.
  • Band plan, including a sub-GHz layer.
  • Microwave or fibre plan, ≥ 99.99% availability for rain zone P.
  • Power plan with ≥ 8 h battery; NSCP 2015 structure; EMF compliance.

After construction

  • Drive test of all 22 barangays within 60 days, with raw logs to the Sanggunian.
  • Annual coverage report for three years.
  • Open access to other operators on published terms.
  • Restoration ≤ 72 h after typhoon damage.
  • Lapses if construction has not started within 12 months.

What the LGU offers

  • Priority one-stop-shop processing.
  • Help finding municipal or barangay land on the chosen ridge, and spur right-of-way.
  • Power-line coordination along the Pinamasingan and Poblacion–Quintina roads.
  • A joint letter to DICT supporting the ridge sites.

Method

What this study can and cannot tell you

Solid

  • The relative comparison. The same model and terrain apply to every site, so "contractor adds nothing, ridges add thousands" holds even if absolute ranges shift.
  • Terrain blocking: the ridges are real and visible in Google Earth's own elevation profile.
  • Calibrated to the council's field facts: four served barangays, no Laurente, 3–4 km reach.

To confirm

  • The contractor's exact coordinates, and the surveyed Bolod tower positions.
  • Actual road and power-line alignments; ours are inferred from terrain.
  • A drive test to replace the calibration with measurements.
  • A site walk at each ridge: SRTM is ±5–10 m, and land ownership matters.
  • The operator's band, power and antenna plan, which sets the real reach.

Analysis code, data, KMZ and every figure: project repository, folders analysis/, data/, results/. Re-run with real coordinates by editing the constants at the top of analysis/contractor_review.py.
